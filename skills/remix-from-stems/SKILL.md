# Remix from stems

The product is the modules and recipes, not one remix. Everything that belongs to one song lives in a song plan file (`plans/NAME.json`), so a second song changes the plan and not the code. The first plan is `plans/friends_in_low_places.json`; it is the regression test for the arranger and the build.

## Inputs

Any song file (WAV, MP3, FLAC). The intake separates it; FL's own "Extract stems" output also works (pass the folder as stems_dir). A base project (an FL-saved stem project, default `sandbox\stemstest\stemstest.flp`, whose channels are replaced), a Sytrus project for fallback patches, and optionally a Serum 2 template project with the person's presets (see `docs/SERUM_MCP.md`). The person's own presets and edits win over defaults: they are the baseline for the next build.

## One command

`python tools/remix_song.py NAME STYLE --song FILE --title "hook line"` runs every step below through the modules and writes `sandbox\remix\NAME_STYLE\remix_report.json`; it renders only while FL is closed. STYLE is a style pack (skill `genre-styles`). Use the steps one by one when a step needs the person. When the brief needs a form the options cannot express, draft the plan with `build_song_plan`, rewrite its sections, clips, chop events and roots (skill `remix-craft`), then run the rest with `--plan EDITED.json`. When the person asks for an auto-tuned vocal or one fitted to the beat, make it first in FL's own editors (skill `gui-fallback`: `quantize_vocal_gui`, then `apply_vocal_tuning_gui`) and pass the result with `--vocals`; a plan's `lead_notes` (from `voiceharmony.lead_rows` on Newtone's MIDI) lets the lead synth double the tuned voice, and `voiceharmony.best_loop` picks a chord loop that holds its notes (`voiceharmony.section_loops` picks one per section kind: skill `song-blueprints`, "The vocal-led layers recipe"). A plan's "pattern_bars": 16 writes one piano roll per section, up to 16 bars, instead of a pattern per bar.

## Creative first

Every song gets its own remix (the person, 2026-10-26: "we don't copy paste formats"; 2026-10-28: "you keep making the same format and same song over and over again"). Read skill `remix-craft` first: it decides the remix's purpose, anchor, how much it keeps, tempo, harmony, form and drops; this skill builds it. Before the plan, write the creative brief (`references/creative-brief.md`): the song's mood (happy, sad, romantic, angry, playful...) with evidence, its energy, sonic space, key, tempo and hook; then choose the style or blend, the palette, the arrangement moves and one surprise particular to this song, each with its reason. The rules below (vocal never slower, whole phrases, whole verses and choruses, editable parts) are constraints. The draft plan `build_song_plan` writes is a starting point: edit its sections, chop games, clips and notes to carry out the brief, and never ship the last song's arrangement unchanged. The plan's form, groove, kit, bass, chords, arp, instruments, transitions, mix and vocal treatment are chosen per song from the option bank the FL demo songs taught (skill `creative-variety`): read the choices, lock the ones the brief needs, and draft again with another seed when they repeat the last remix.

## Steps

1. **Intake.** Stems come from FL Studio's own "Extract stems from sample" (the person, 2026-10-29: FL already does this): `extract_stems_gui` writes a sandbox project with the song on playlist track 1 (`prepare_stems_project`), opens it (FL is started when closed; a running FL with unsaved work stops at its Save question, so ask), runs the clip menu entry and copies the four stems as vocals, drums, bass and other into `sandbox\songs\NAME\stems\fl`. Then `build_song_analysis` (A2, GPU) with stems_dir set to that folder: the song as WAV (the local Mel-Band RoFormer and Demucs models, `separate_stems_hq`, are the fallback when FL cannot run, `--separator models`), the bar grid from beat_this (`detect_beats_ml`: tempo by a line through the beats, meter, drift), the words (Whisper at the pinned revision), the sung melody (`transcribe_vocal_melody`), the drum entrance, the bass roots per bar, a vote of three key estimates and the song's own sections. Its "ask" list (a split key vote, a drifting tempo, a meter other than 4/4) goes to the person before planning.
1b. **Research the song** (before planning; the person asked for it on 2026-10-25). Name it from `find_song_clues` (its file name, its title and artist tags, the line its transcript repeats most, as search queries) and confirm by web search. Then read current sources on its form (intro, verses, choruses, solos, the ending), its story and **mood** (what the lyrics are about and how they feel: happy, sad, romantic, angry, defiant, comic...), and the parts fans love most (the sing-along, a famous intro, a solo, a live-only verse), and save them with `write_song_notes` as song_notes.json in the song folder, each favourite with what the remix does with it and its source link. Never copy lyrics from the web: the words come from the person's own file through Whisper, and `write_song_notes` refuses a note that repeats six words of the transcript in a row. Look too for a reference MIDI of the song (MIDI World and sites like it publish transcriptions with piano, bass, guitars and drums); after the person approves the download (source and size), fetch it into the song folder with `tools/fetch_reference_midi.py`. It is only a baseline: step 2 tests it against the record.
2. **Plan.** `build_song_plan` with options.style, bass_file, vocals_file (the vocal stem, for where nobody sings), notes_file, midi_files and variety (default on: seed, lock, moods; the history in sandbox/variety_history.json keeps the next remix different). The arrangement follows the song's own form (`songform`): the song's parts found from its vocal (a chorus starts where the title line follows a breath, choruses repeat at the first chorus's length, the vocal stem's silences mark the intro and the solo; when the title opens no chorus, `songform.form_by_repeats`: what the song sings again is chorus, what it sings once is verse, and with a one-word title the lines it ends; with no bass line in the record the roots come from the harmony stem, `songform.harmony_roots`), then **every verse plays whole** over a groove, **every chorus plays whole** over a drop with eight bars of chop games after it, the instrumental parts between play the **original's instrumentation** (the Demucs "other" stem: guitars, keys, steel) and the pack's intro, builds and outro go around them. The vocal plays at **its own tempo or faster, never slower** (`stylepack.pick_tempo`: the tempo in the style's range that speeds it up least; a rap beat may sit at the song's own tempo; a plan with a slower vocal fails validation). Chops are **whole sung lines**, never single words or pieces: the hook is the couplet (the title line and the line sung straight after it, up to 12 s, a slurred pickup like "'cause" left off), then the chorus's other complete lines, and one hook syllable for the stutter. From the person's edit of the techno remix (2026-10-26): the hook a cappella on beat 2 of the intro, a line answering into each drop from the build's end, and the build before the last drop carrying the verse on into the chorus and ending on a seven-hit stutter (`songform.STUTTER_HITS`); every second build counting back from it carries the verse through its first half, then the line answers and has finished as the drop comes in. Each chop keeps the song's beat phase (`remixbuild.chop_phase`): it starts as far after a remix beat as its first word sits after a beat of the song, so the beat inside it lands on the remix's beat (the person moved every chop by that much on The Curse of Curves). No chop is transposed. `--every-part` keeps every verse and every chorus (the person's ask for The Curse of Curves). A reference MIDI is used only when it passes `midiref.fit` (its bass roots agree with the record's on 60% of bars at its best offset and transposition, 80% of its notes in the record's key); then the chord synth plays the song's chords (each root's quality as the file plays it below E4, so its melody does not blur them) in the file's strum, voiced as close triads or bare fifths between A3 and A4, never denser. Refused files are listed with the reason in the plan's midi_candidates. The bass follows the record's own roots bar by bar where a remix bar carries a source bar. It is a draft: read its notes and show the form, the kept parts and the chops to the person (V1). The first song's hand plan is `plans/friends_in_low_places.json` (tech house, the regression build).
3. **Audio.** `prepare_remix_audio` (other_path: the "other" stem when the plan plays it): the vocal stretched to the remix tempo (rubberband, formants kept), each long clip and chop cut on syllable onsets and carried through its last word (a phrase end runs to where the voice stops and through a closing s or z: `cutrefine.voice_end`, `final_s`; a cut followed straight on by the next word stops at that word's onset), the style's kit and bass sample, the FX bed. Vocal cutting rules: `editable-first` and its `vocal-chops` reference; check with `check_vocal_edges` and `find_final_s_end`. Write a new audio folder, never into an earlier one.
3b. **Sounds.** Each synth part the remix plays (chords, sub, arp, a lead) gets its own Serum 2 sound matched to the part of the original it answers, never the last song's preset: `measure_part_sound` on that stem, the Serum MCP's `generate_preset` from its draft (subfolder `sandbox/<song>`), `render_serum_sound` and `compare_part_sound` until it belongs to the record, then the creative move the brief names (`sound-design-recipes`, "Choosing the sound"). The states Serum saved go to the build as options.serum_states. `match_part_sound` runs that loop for one part (three rounds at most, the round nearest the original kept), and `python tools/remix_song.py ... --match-sounds` runs it for the stab and arp (from the other stem) and the sub (from the bass stem) before the build, reporting what each sound still misses. `--match-to PART=SOURCE` changes the stem a part is matched to: `vocals` when the person wants the synths made from the voice, `none` to keep the plan's library sound (a remake that must not keep the record's tone). A part with a sound choice in the plan passes its measured demo instrument as options.direction: the original's tone with that instrument's envelope and width.
4. **Build.** `build_remix_project` writes a sandbox copy: drums as sampler channels with patterns, bass and sub, the chord synth and arp as channels with notes, chops as sampler channels, the stretched vocal, the original's instrumentation ("Original Instruments", its own insert, on the music bus) and the FX bed as playlist audio, a time marker at each section. Synths are **Serum 2 when available**: a part with a generated sound (step 3b, options.serum_states) gets it on a Serum 2 channel; the others are cloned from options.serum_project, a project holding Serum 2 channels with the person's sounds (by preset name; the person's own choices so far: "CP - Deep Sub", "PL Crusty Pluck [LCV]" for chords, "ARP - Jno Style"), Sytrus only when there is none. A part the style does not use gets no channel. The plan's kit (a demo song's samples) replaces the synthesized one-shots, and a part with no generated Serum sound plays the plan's demo instrument, imported from its render copy in its own register. Options: `solo`, `kit`, `organize` (default on: `docs/PROJECT_STANDARD.md`), demo_dir, record_variety (default on: the choices go into the history). For a project that was not built here, `apply_project_standard` does the same into a sandbox copy.
5. **Produce.** `apply_production_recipe` with the style's recipe and plan_file (the plan's production overrides: its transitions, pump and buses from the variety choices) ("club_house" for house and tech house; `techno`, `dubstep`, `dnb`, `trap`): buses, effect chains by role, the pump, transitions at every section change by the kinds of the two sections, the house standard. Read the steps it could not apply: each says why. Each move is also a module (`create_bus_routing`, `apply_fx_chain`, `build_volume_pump`, `add_transition_automation`, `add_transition_synths`) for a project that needs only some of it.
6. **Render and shape.** Only while FL is closed (`render_via_cli` refuses otherwise; `render_with_restart` is A1). `measure_section_levels` gives each drop's contrast and every section's level. Two rules, turned together each round with `apply_section_gains` and rendered again: every drop sits 3 to 5.5 LU over its build (by_role scaled; contrast moves about 7.5 LU per unit of scale on the first five genres, so aim from the slope), and no intro, groove, break or outro sits further under the drops than the targets' section floor (6 LU: 7 left dubstep's loudness range at 8.46 against the 8 LU limit), raised on the master insert by section kind. The first five genre renders had intros and breaks 10 to 14 LU down and loudness ranges of 10 to 14 LU; club releases average 6.6.
7. **Gain stage by part** when the balance is off: `split_part_projects`, `render_folder_via_cli`, `measure_part_levels`, `compare_part_levels` (skill `gain-staging`).
8. **Master.** `apply_club_master` to the style's targets file (club release: -8 LUFS +/- 1, true peak at most -0.5 dBTP, crest at least 6.5). It first removes DC, makes the lows mono below the targets' low_end_mono_below_hz, and cuts a tail that went silent (a held offset once kept FL rendering 33 s past the song's end), then gain into a soft clipper and a look-ahead limiter, measured again independently; a loudness the crest floor forbids is reported, not forced (skill `mastering`). With the person's reference tracks in `sandbox\references\STYLE`, `build_reference_profile` measures them once into a profile and a targets file of their own (their median loudness, their crest and loudness range), and the master also moves broadly toward their tonal balance (only what lies outside their spread). `tools/remix_song.py ... --finish` redoes steps 8 and 9 on an earlier run, so new references need no new render.
9. **Judge.** `check_remix_panel` (A1): every target limit on the master, its length against the plan, the contrast and any DC offset held in silence on the mixdown (`measure_dc_offset`: a held offset is a stuck plugin state in the project, so the recipe gets fixed; a one-sided kick only moves the file's mean, which the master removes), the tonal balance against the reference profile, how loud the voice is in the first drop by the vocal separation model (a vocal more than 20 LU under the excerpt counts as buried; a first guess to calibrate), how close it sounds to the references against the original as a baseline (`compare_to_references_ml`), and the critic graded on questions whose answers are known (the rhythm from the style, the voice from the separation). An untrusted critic is listed and never counted. Its "problems" stand, its "ask_person" goes to the person, its notes are not acted on. The person listens last (V1).
10. **Learn from their fixes.** When the person edits and saves a copy, run `diff_flp_projects` on the build and the copy. For each finding change the recipe or the style pack, or log it as song specific in `docs/LESSONS_TO_RECIPES.md`. Never rebuild over their file.
11. **Check timing by measurement.** `measure_part_timing` on a solo render of one channel reports the median offset from the grid; fix the sound or the recipe, not the clip.

## Rules

- The person's remix rules (2026-10-25, changed 2026-10-28): the vocal at its own tempo, faster, or at most 6 % slower with its pitch and formants kept; chops are whole phrases in the song's natural language; lines stay whole and no word is cut, while which lines, their order and their role are chosen per remix (before 2026-10-28: every verse and chorus whole, in order); the original's instrumentation where it serves, and cutting games only between lines; a reference MIDI is a tested baseline for the chords and cadence; chords are simplified to triads or roots wherever they would crowd.

- Editable first: audio only for vocals, FX, long textures and resamples.
- Never close FL, close a project or render without asking. Never write the person's originals; outputs sit under a folder named `sandbox`.
- Say what could not be verified. A tool that reports success is not a listening result.
- When the sound is wrong, change the preset or the recipe. Do not nudge notes to hide it.
- A recipe change must keep the first plan's build identical: run the tests before and after.
