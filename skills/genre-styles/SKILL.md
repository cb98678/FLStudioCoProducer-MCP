# Genre styles

A genre is a style pack, `styles/NAME.json`: data, not code. The remix pipeline reads it for the tempo, the section template, the drum, bass, sub and chord patterns, the drum kit and bass sample it synthesizes, the production recipe (`recipes/production/NAME.json`) and the targets file. Packs today: "tech_house" (the first song's engine, kept as code for the regression build), `house`, "deep_house", "nu_disco", `techno`, `dubstep`, `dnb`, `trap` and "hiphop" (boom bap), and fifteen from the FL demo study (2026-10-28, `docs/DEMO_STUDY.md`): "afro_house", `afrobeats`, `reggaeton`, `trance`, "melodic_techno", "liquid_dnb", "future_bass", "uk_drill", `lofi`, "trip_hop", "pop_dance", "future_house", "big_room", "electro_industrial" and `cinematic`, each composed from the option bank and citing its demo song, and "big_beat" (2026-10-29, the first No Sleep Till Brooklyn remix: a funky breakbeat, a rock bass, an acid line on its own Serum sound, the record's riffs cut back in, a tape stop out). Each cites where its numbers come from. Production recipes: "club_house", "deep_house", "nu_disco", `techno`, `dubstep`, `dnb`, `trap`, `hiphop`, and from the demo study `trance` (long filter openings, a 16-bar build, the pump on the bass alone), "afro_house" (the filter is the build, swells and throws, no risers), `lofi` (entries and exits, tape stops), `cinematic` (no pump, swells and one hit) "latin_pop" (the dembow drops out for a beat before the chorus) and "big_beat" (low-pass openings over the builds, a gap and an impact into each drop, tape stops into the break and the outro); each song's mix, fx and automation choices are merged over its pack's recipe (skill `creative-variety`).

A pack is the genre's centre, not the remix: on top of it every song gets its own form, groove, kit, bass line, chord treatment, arp, instruments, transitions and mix from the option bank (skill `creative-variety`), so two remixes in one genre do not sound alike.

## Pick the style

0. **From the song, not from habit.** Read its mood and sonic space first (`remix-from-stems`, `references/creative-brief.md`). Every pack lists the moods it serves ("moods"), and `stylepack.for_mood` ranks the packs that share the song's mood words (the draft plan writes them into its notes and its brief). A starting map, to argue with per song: happy, euphoric, playful: nu-disco (pack "nu_disco"), funky, piano or French filter house; sad, bittersweet, romantic: deep or melodic house (pack "deep_house"), progressive, organic house; romantic, sensual: deep house, UK garage, R&B house; angry, defiant: techno, hard techno, bass house, dubstep; playful, comic: bass house, ghetto house, jersey club; nostalgic: French house, disco edits; dark, anxious: techno, minimal, drum and bass. A pack that does not exist yet is a reason to add one ("Add a genre" below), not to force the nearest. The person's words still win ("club mix" asks for a club style, the mood picks which). The demo study adds: sexy, party, hot: reggaeton; sunny, laid back: afrobeats; tribal, spiritual: afro house; euphoric, epic: trance or big room; hypnotic, dark: melodic techno; soulful, melancholic: liquid drum and bass; bittersweet, emotional: future bass; menacing, cold: UK drill or electro industrial; nostalgic, calm: lo-fi; moody, sensual: trip hop; mysterious, suspense: cinematic.
1. Map the person's words to a pack: "club mix" is house or tech house unless they say more; "tech house" for any song is "tech_house_groove" (the grammar engine, so the variety bank can change its form, groove, bass and chords; added 2026-10-29 for Open Your Heart), never "tech_house", which is the first song's fixed engine kept only so the Friends regression build stays identical; "rap mix" is "hiphop" (boom bap, 85 to 95 bpm) or `trap` when they say trap (a beat under the original vocal; a new verse is their own recording, never a cloned voice); "dubstep", "techno", "drum and bass" name their packs. Ask only when two packs fit equally.
2. The tempo comes from the pack, under the person's rule (2026-10-25, changed 2026-10-28): **the vocal plays at its own tempo, faster, or at most 6 % slower, its pitch and formants kept** ("yes but not too slow and preserve pitch"; before, never slower, after a vocal slowed 17 to 20 % sounded "long and drawn out" on the hip-hop and drum and bass remixes). `stylepack.pick_tempo` keeps the song's own tempo (or its half or double) when it lies in the pack's range; otherwise it takes the tempo in the range that changes the vocal least (a slowdown counts 1.5 times a speed-up of the same size), with the vocal multiple that does the same (`stylepack.vocal_multiple`). A speed-up above 1.35x falls back to the song's own tempo when the pack follows (follow true); a pack that must stay at one tempo (drum and bass, follow false) keeps it and the plan notes the speed-up. On Friends in Low Places (108.5 bpm): techno 126 (x1.16), house 118, dubstep 138, trap 130, hip-hop at the song's own 108.5, drum and bass 170 with the vocal at 1.57x. `songplan.validate` refuses a plan whose vocal plays more than 6 % slower than sung.
3. Run the pipeline with the style: `build_song_plan` with options.style, then `prepare_remix_audio`, `build_remix_project`, `apply_production_recipe` with the pack's production recipe (or `python tools/remix_song.py NAME STYLE` for all of it, see `remix-from-stems`).

## What a pack holds

- `tempo`: bpm, range and follow (default true: when the song's own tempo, or its half or double, lies in the range it is kept and the vocal is not stretched; false for a genre played at one tempo). `sections`: [kind, bars] with kinds intro, groove, break, build, drop, outro.
- `drums`: per section kind, `bars` with one pattern per voice (kick, snare, clap, hat, ohat, shaker, rim, tom, crash, perc, ride; perc and ride get a channel only when a plan plays them), `last` for the section's last bar (a pre-drop mute), "fill_every" and `fill`, `roll` "accelerate" for a build's snare roll, `ramp` [start, end] velocity scale; "swing" (0.5 straight to 0.75, MPC-style) delays every off 16th (afro house percussion sits at 0.66, a third of a 16th late).
- Patterns: 16 characters per bar of 16ths (32 for 32nds, 8 for 8ths, 12 and 24 for triplet 8ths and 16ths). Drums: X accent, x normal, o soft. Bass, sub and chords: x root, X accented root, o octave up, 5 fifth, v the fifth below, 4 the fourth, 7 the minor seventh, 3 the chord's third, b the flat second, d an octave down, - holds, . rests. A list of patterns plays one bar after another.
- `bass` (the sampler bass, the reese or growl or 808), `sub` (the Sytrus or Serum sub), each with an optional "pedal" list of section kinds that hold the tonic; `chords` (stabs or pads) with an optional "voicing" (triad, seventh, add9, sus2, sus4, open, octaves, minmaj, scale_run) and "top_only"; `arp`: a list of section kinds for the rising arp, or an object {"shape" (rise, fall, updown, pedal, octave, broken, tresillo, chromatic, sequence), "rate", "sections", "octaves", "double", "rhythm", "cell", "gate"}. A role with no patterns gets no channel.
- "form" (optional): the arrangement policy a form option sets (which choruses get a build, the chop-game tail, a bridge, a gap before the drop, drum drop-outs, marker labels); see `creative-variety`.
- "levels_db": the style's starting mixer levels by group, ahead of the Serum sound levels (house: the person's master B balance). "kick_sub_shelf": {hz, db}, a low shelf on the generated kick (house: 60 Hz, -4.5 dB).
- `kit`: kit file names to new voices from `oneshots.VOICE_FNS` (kick, snare, rumble_kick, bass_808, growl, reese_pluck and the rest) with arguments. "bass_sample": the voice for CP_Reese.wav and its channel label.
- "production_recipe", `targets` (today `targets/club_release.toml` for every club style), `sources`.
- "moods": the song moods the pack serves (deep house: sad, romantic, bittersweet, nostalgic; nu-disco: happy, playful, comic, flirty, euphoric). A new pack names its moods.

## Add a genre

1. Research it from current sources (tempo, drum positions, bass design, arrangement lengths, mix and loudness), cite each in `sources`, and mark what is opinion.
2. Write the pack and a production recipe; `stylepack.validate` and `production.check_recipe` must be clean (tests/test_stylepack.py loads every pack and builds a project from the first song's plan with it).
3. Build the first song in the new style, render, and judge it with `check_remix_panel`; the person listens before the pack counts as done.

## Rules

- Club packs (house, tech house, techno, dubstep, dnb) open with 16 or 32 bars a DJ can mix: kick, hats or percussion, minimal bass, no full melody or vocal ([Lost Stories Academy](https://loststoriesacademy.com/blogs-and-tutorials/what-makes-a-track-dj-friendly)). The first dnb and dubstep intros had hats only and sat 12 to 14 LU under the drops. Outros stay steady to mix out of (drum ramp no lower than 0.85): a fade to 0.4 pulled house's loudness range to 8.6 LU and needed an 11 dB break lift to compensate.

- Numbers in a pack are starting points from sources; the person's reference tracks override them.
- Do not change the "tech_house" engine to fix another genre; the Friends regression build must stay identical. A new song in tech house uses "tech_house_groove".
- Editable first: every part stays a pattern on a channel; the synthesized bass sample is tuned to A1 on key 60 so bass lines use the same keys in every style.
